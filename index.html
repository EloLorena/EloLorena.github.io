Dr Eloísa Lorena Ruiz Pérez

Serra Húnter Professor Lector · Department of Applied Physics, Faculty of Physics, Universitat de Barcelona
Visiting scientist · Molecular Bionics Group, Institute for Bioengineering of Catalonia (IBEC)

I measure the structure, dynamics and mechanics of soft and biological nano-objects directly in water, using liquid-phase transmission electron microscopy (LP-TEM), image analysis and multiscale simulation. My current focus is the mechanics of amyloid-β and tau assemblies and the architecture of supramolecular nanomedicines for Alzheimer's disease.

About

After graduating in Physics at the University of Seville (2001), I developed my academic career in the UK from 2002 to 2021: a PhD in soft-matter physics at the University of Sheffield (2006), followed by academic and industry-sponsored postdoctoral work carried out in departments of engineering materials, chemistry and biomedical science. In 2014 I moved to University College London, where from 2016 to 2021 I set up and managed the EPSRC/JEOL Centre for Liquid-Phase TEM, the first of its kind in the UK and one of the first in Europe. There we developed LP-TEM of polymer assemblies and proteins, as well as Brownian tomography and open image-analysis tools.

In 2021, after two decades in the UK, I returned to Spain and joined IBEC as senior researcher and deputy group leader in the Molecular Bionics Group, received the BIST fellowship "To the Mothers of Science" (2022) and became PI of the AEI project FaSIMProD (2023). Since February 2024 I have been Serra Húnter Professor Lector at the Universitat de Barcelona, where I lead an independent research line in LP-TEM.

My career has not been linear: two maternity leaves (2009, 2013) and part-time teaching as primary caregiver amount to about 4.8 years of research interruption.

Research

Liquid-phase electron microscopy

Fast, low-dose LP-TEM to image soft and biological matter in its hydrated state, at nanometre and millisecond resolution.

Mechanics of misfolded proteins

Flicker spectroscopy of LP-TEM videos gives the bending rigidity and interfacial tension of amyloid-β and tau oligomers in liquid.

Supramolecular nanomedicine

Structure determines function: architecture, ligand distribution and mechanics of polymer vesicles and micelles that target LRP1 at the blood-brain barrier.

Image analysis and simulation

Open-source pipelines (SimpliPyTEM) and atomistic, coarse-grained and Monte Carlo models compared with the measured data.
